Original work & editorial choices

Inner Sleeve Archive is a student music appreciation project by Diancheng Jiang. The artist selection, listening route and visual direction connect creative context with specific details to hear. The record icon, page structure, listening prompts and interactive games were developed for this website.

AI tools supported research organization and early drafting, English language polishing, HTML, CSS and JavaScript preparation, and troubleshooting. The homepage image three-artists-gatefold.png is an AI-generated composite illustration based on the approved three-person design; it is not a documentary photograph of the three artists together.

Release information and short quotations link to their sources. Listening commentary reflects editorial interpretation and invites visitors to notice musical choices. Full song lyrics are not reproduced.

Article headings and body text use Arial or Helvetica. Quotations use Georgia italics; Collection headings and names use regular Georgia. The Home title, card descriptions and original white wordmark use DM Sans ↗ . Its SIL Open Font License is included with the local font files.

The Listening Room

The thirteen MP3 recordings and their embedded cover images were supplied by the project creator, rather than downloaded from the reference links on this page. The Listening Room and artist-page dialogs play the same local files through the browser’s audio element. Permission to redistribute those supplied recordings was not independently verified; release citations document music facts, not an audio licence.

The Best Part file carries the H.E.R. compilation cover and is identified accordingly in the player. The Acura Integurl file carries The Lonny Breaux Collection artwork and album tag; these identify the supplied file’s metadata. An official release edition and date were not independently verified, so the player does not assign a release year to this recording.

The player covers named record-* in the images folder were extracted from the supplied MP3 metadata. The artist-page album illustrations came from the separate supplied image folder. Neither process establishes new rights in the artwork.
