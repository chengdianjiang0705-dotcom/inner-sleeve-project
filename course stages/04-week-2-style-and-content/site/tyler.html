03 / The artist studies

Tyler, The Creator

Bright arrangements, private doubts.

Tyler, the Creator is the professional name of Tyler Gregory Okonma, born on March 6, 1991, in Hawthorne, California. An American rapper, singer, record producer and fashion designer, he connects his albums with distinctive characters, visual identities and clothing projects.

Tyler helped form Odd Future in 2007 and independently released Bastard in 2009. Goblin (2011), together with the video for Yonkers, brought wider attention to his solo career. Wolf (2013) and Cherry Bomb (2015) expanded his music through jazz, soul and R&B influences.

Flower Boy (2017) includes November, See You Again and 911 / Mr. Lonely. IGOR (2019) and CALL ME IF YOU GET LOST (2021) each won the Grammy for Best Rap Album. Later releases include CHROMAKOPIA (2024) and Don't Tap the Glass (2025), continuing a career that also includes directing videos and founding the Golf Wang and Le Fleur fashion brands.

FROM THE RECORD

Flower Boy

2017 / Focus track: November

Listen on NetEase ↗

How do the repeated musical setting and the other speakers' memories turn November from a month into an idea of safety or belonging?

November begins by placing questions and worries inside an inviting musical setting. The repeating accompaniment has a settled quality, but the speaker's thoughts keep moving toward things that could go wrong. I hear a useful tension between those two layers. The music offers a place to rest while the voice keeps checking whether that place, and the life around it, will last.

The recurring reference to November gradually becomes larger than a date. In my reading, it works as shorthand for a time when life felt easier to understand. The return of the hook gives that idea a musical form: the song keeps circling back to a remembered place. Repetition does not recover the past, but it lets the listener feel the strength of the wish to return.

The spoken memories change who gets to define that place. Other voices enter with their own answers, so the song briefly opens beyond Tyler's account. Their different memories suggest that an ideal time cannot be reduced to one shared event. I hear the interlude as a small gathering inside the recording, making nostalgia social even when the surrounding questions remain personal.

Listen again for the moment the spoken voices interrupt the familiar musical flow. The shift in delivery matters as much as the stories themselves: speech sounds less arranged than a hook, even though it has been carefully placed here. For me, the contrast gives the record the feeling of a saved conversation. November becomes somewhere the song can revisit, without pretending that the past can be restored.

On your next listen

See You Again (feat. Kali Uchis)

Flower Boy / 2017

Listen on NetEase ↗
Why this song
The clear melodic hook offers an easy first route into Flower Boy. It also introduces the way Tyler gives different voices different roles, allowing a guest singer to expand the emotional space around his own delivery.
Listen for
Listen to the change between Tyler's phrases and Kali Uchis's melodic replies. Then follow the keyboard and larger instrumental gestures. I hear the arrangement making imagined closeness feel almost tangible, while the distance between voices keeps the longing in view.

EARFQUAKE

IGOR / 2019

Listen on NetEase ↗
Why this song
A repeated plea and a memorable keyboard figure make this a direct entry into IGOR. Comparing it with See You Again shows how the voice can become one part of a deliberately textured arrangement, rather than sit clearly above everything.
Listen for
Notice the slightly unstable keyboard sound and the way the vocal layers blend with it. The groove remains inviting while the speaker asks someone to stay. I hear the wavering texture as part of that dependence, giving the repeated request a fragile edge.

911 / Mr. Lonely

Flower Boy / 2017

Listen on NetEase ↗
Why this song
Two connected sections make the song a useful introduction to contrast within one track. The inviting first part and more insistent later passage approach loneliness from different angles, letting arrangement become part of the argument.
Listen for
Find the turn between the two sections and compare how the voice occupies each beat. The first part leaves room for melody; the second brings the rap forward more forcefully. To me, the switch exposes a pressure that the earlier groove holds at a distance.
